The Kalyanamitra

About

A correspondence between strangers by post.  Each week, one or more letters are answered.  All correspondence is handwritten or typed. The querent’s and the kalyanamitra’s reply are published here, with all identifying information removed.  This site is only for eliciting letters and sending them off, like messages in a bottle, into the world at large. What begins in private becomes public but not publicity. Kalyanamitra-letters.com serves no commercial purpose.  No names, no addresses, no photographs, no social media, no email, no newsletter, no cookies, no membership fee.  Just words between friends.  And, everyone is welcome.

What

Writing a letter offers an opportunity to know yourself better.  That gift is the gift of your words to you.  If you choose to post the letter, I commit to reading it and possibly sending a reply: a considerate, handwritten or typed letter, mailed back and also published here.  Responses will be grounded in contemplative practice and direct experience, not theory or doctrine, though they will inevitably be informed by those, too.

I will not speak to what I do not know.  I might even write to tell you so that I am not the one to give an answer.  If a response from my experience and understanding is not possible, I offer my silence and prayers: no making things up.  I am a fellow journeyer.  I’ll do my best.  I won’t tell you what to do.  I’ll encourage and share from my heart.

What is not offered: therapy, medical advice, crisis intervention, or formal spiritual instruction. If you are in crisis, please contact a mental health professional or a crisis line (in the United States, call or text 988).

Letters are our only form of contact.  Why?  Because the internet is excellent at speed and terrible at depth.  Because slowness is a form of respect, and because we move too much at the speed of thought and not enough at the speed of our bodies.  Because the body has its own wisdom and it, too, wants to speak and be heard.  Because a letter requires you to sit with what you want to say and knowledge actually forms in the writing or manually typing words out on a page.  Because a reply requires that I choose to give you my time and sit with what you have said.  Because our conversation may make us wiser, kinder people in real life (and this is real life).  Because this project is small by design and wants to stay small.

To write to the Kalyanamitra, read the instructions.

Why

We journey through life toward death, arriving along the different routes that are our lived lives. Some roads are long, some short. Some easy, others steep. This project’s aspiration is to be a space for one dharma friend to be in dialogue with others, writing to the world with field reports of our individual and collective experiences.  Charting the territory as we go, sharing our experiences, we can support one another and, leaving a record, we can even benefit fellow travellers.  

While our experiences are uniquely our own, they are equally universal.  Our suffering, pains, challenges, like our joys and triumphs, are ultimately of one taste.  A question asked in private often contains something universal. Someone else, reading our correspondence, may recognize themselves in the words and feel less alone, less confused, less in doubt.  Because the tradition of correspondence has always been, in part, a public performance — think of Abelard and Heloise, Rilke’s letters to the young poet, Seneca’s letters to Lucilius (your friend makes no claims to greatness, but finds inspiration in those who are great).  What starts as a private exchange becomes a source of companionship for readers far away in place and time.

We are constantly interacting with dead words rather than the living, beautifully vibrant expressions of beautiful, living beings. The deluge of email, instant messages, social media and more — passing at the speed of electrons in and out of consciousness and out of date by the time they're sent — isolates us. They turn us into consumers, into an audience, into something to be monetized.  And an AI model may demonstrate intelligence and engage us in “conversation,” but an LLM (large language model) literally cannot be aware, and so we pretend to converse with what are, at best, echoes off the cliff of our culture.

The Kalyanamitra has benefitted so much from the correspondence and writing of other practitioners: Buddhist, Christian, Muslim, Indigenous (a too broad word encapsulating wildly different, marginalized traditions), agnostic.  He humbly offers this service to carry on the spirit of dialogue.  May this be a small shelter from the storm of the world, wherever you are and wherever you go.

To everyone who comes to this site and lingers, thank you. You, too, are a kalyanamitra.  Together we are sangha, a sacred community, even if we are also largely strangers.  As I hope to be a support for you, your presence is a support for me.  May all beings find happiness and the causes of happiness.  May all beings find freedom and the causes of freedom.  May our collected words serve as beneficent presences in these difficult times.

Who

Kalyanamitra: a spiritual friend — someone who meets you on the path not as a teacher but as a fellow journeyer; not as an authority but as someone walking his own spiritual path and sharing experiences and wisdom picked up along the way. A kalyanamitra listens and replies with as much compassion and wisdom born from practice as they can muster — to help us to find our own answers, or at least better understand our questions.

Having asked and been asked many questions over the years, the kalyanamitra has learned that the best answers come slowly, in his case on paper, where there is time to think and no expectation of results.

Specifically, this kalyanamitra is a yoga practitioner and meditator and a teacher of both. He has sat with his own confusion and pain, been lost and found and lost again, and believes that the most valuable thing one person can offer another is not advice but wisdom, attention and inspiration.

Specific details — names, teachers, institutions, locations, dates of practice — have been left out intentionally. This site is about words as tools to ignite our inner light(s) and help dispell the darkness of ignorance. It is about mutual care and respect for ourselves, each other and the world. It is about service to others as the best path toward self-realization.

In service,
