Write

All handwritten letters and those written with a typewriter are read. Mail letters to the address below. The kalyanamitra reads all submissions and responds to at least one each week, also in writing or by typewriter.

A handcrafted letter is of a different kind than modern mass and instant communication.  It’s more intentional, asking us to slow down and consider what really wants to be said.  Manual writing lets us hold a thought long enough to shape it into words, like raw clay taking shape in our hands.  Opening a letter from a friend, you already know it’s not spam, an advertisement, or AI-slop.  These letters are something real that you can touch: it’s something written by one embodied being and intended just for another.

Write to me, but write for yourself.  This is not just about the reply; it is something bigger. If or when a reply comes, it won’t come quickly. You are invited into a conversation that happens on its own time.  Our connection could take days or weeks or months.  And there is risk: we do not know one another; mail gets lost; my reply may not satisfy; much life will unfold as our letters physically travel our country or even the globe, changing you and changing me and enriching our conversation with what has transpired.  This distance actually creates a greater immediacy between us.  

When you are ready, write. Take your time. There’s no rush. The post box is there if you choose to mail your letter. Life is short — but you have all the time in the world (reincarnation is a thing).

Write about . . .

How you live your practice and how your practice lives in you. What questions are in your heart and in your bones regarding your spiritual path? What needs to be given voice or allowed to speak for itself?  What inspires or challenges your practice, meditation, prayer, &c that you want to discuss?  What lives in you right now?  What wants to be shared with another human being? This project was formed in a Buddhist context but is not limited to Buddhist practice or even to Buddhists.  Some possibilities include  

  • integration of practice and daily life
  • the afflictive emotions of anger, attachment, greed, pride and ignorance
  • Relationships as practice
  • meditation — what is difficult, what is opening, what frustrates, seems impossible or distracts
  • the power and challenges of prayer, mantra or devotion
  • practicing among those who do not
  • the feeling of being stuck, moving too quickly, or even going backwards
  • a passage of text that troubles, puzzles or otherwise gnaws at you
  • what you are afraid of or hoping for in your practice

If you want to write and don’t need a reply, write about whatever you like. Write about your garden, what makes you laugh or cry or your grief, a favorite teaching or poem. I’ll read and witness it all.

You don’t need to be a Buddhist. You need no faith or religion. You can be an atheist or agnostic (once upon a time so was I), preferably one with a contemplative practice given the intention of this endeavor. Everyone with a question about mind or spirit and how to live in this world is welcome here.

The Process

The post box gets checked regularly. Each week, at least one letter is answered physically and posted on this site. Additional letters are answered as time allows.

Replies are typewritten (occasionally handwritten) and mailed to the return address included in your letter.  This is not a rapid exchange.  If you send a letter, expect to wait several weeks, or more, to hear back.  Let this slowness be part of the practice, a part of the experience.  Everything sent is kept, and if something haunts me or triggers me long after reading, I may return to a letter weeks after the initial reading to reply.  Nothing is lost. Our lives are an infinite conversation with all that is; let’s not rush things.

Together, your letter (with all personal information redacted) and my reply will be scanned and published on the Letters page. This is the process.

Include with your letter

An address: a reply demands one.  Include it on the first page of your letter.  Your return address will be kept private and never published. If you do not wish a reply, then no address is needed.

Names are welcome, but if you prefer to be anonymous (as I’ve chosen to be), sign yourself, “a friend.”

Please no threatening or abusive words directed to me or about others. None of us need to give or receive that kind of karma in our lives.

Consent

Sending a letter is consent to its publication on this site and, possibly, in physical form. If you do not wish your letter to be published, you might still write, but don’t send it. Posting is consent.  

Important

This is not a crisis service. If you are in immediate danger, or experiencing a mental health emergency, please contact emergency services or a crisis line in your country. In the United States, you can call or text 988 for the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline. I am not a therapist and not a medical professional. In such cases, all I can offer are prayers.  These letters are not a substitute for professional care.
